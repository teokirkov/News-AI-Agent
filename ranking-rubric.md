# Ranking Rubric

Used to score and order candidate stories after filtering. Not meant to be
mathematically precise — a lightweight way to keep ranking consistent
across runs rather than arbitrary.

## Score each candidate 1–5 on these dimensions

1. **Scope of impact** — How many people/countries/markets are affected?
   - 5 = global or multi-national impact
   - 3 = national impact
   - 1 = local/niche impact

2. **Severity/significance** — How consequential is it?
   - 5 = life-and-death, major policy shift, historic event
   - 3 = notable but not transformative
   - 1 = minor development, incremental update

3. **Recency/momentum** — How fresh and actively developing is it?
   - 5 = breaking within last 1–2 days, still unfolding
   - 3 = within the week, relatively settled
   - 1 = right at the edge of the 7-day window, mostly resolved

4. **Source strength & breadth** — How well corroborated is it?
   - 5 = 4–5 independent credible outlets confirming, spanning different
     editorial leans
   - 3 = 2–3 credible sources confirming
   - 1 = only 1 credible source found (still eligible, but scores low
     here — must carry a coverage note per CLAUDE.md Rule 3)

## Total score
- Add the four scores (max 20).
- Rank candidates highest to lowest total.
- Take the top 10.

## Tie-breakers
- Prefer topical diversity over a second/third story on the same event,
  unless one event is genuinely dominating the week's news.
- Between two similarly-scored stories, prefer the one with stronger,
  more independent, and more ideologically varied source corroboration.

## A note on source count vs. importance
- A story shouldn't be excluded just because it only has 1–2 credible
  sources — genuinely important but under-reported stories are worth
  surfacing, with the limited coverage flagged.
- However, all else being equal, a well-corroborated story with varied
  source lean is a stronger, more reliable inclusion than a single-source
  story — this is reflected in dimension 4 above.

## Sanity check before finalizing
- Do the top 10 feel like what a well-informed person would consider "the
  week's big stories" — not niche, not stale, not redundant?
- Is there at least some topical spread unless justified by the week's
  actual news?
- Across the whole report, is the source lean reasonably varied overall
  (not every single story sourced from outlets with the same lean)?
